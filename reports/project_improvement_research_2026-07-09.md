## Part B — Is the current pipeline methodology optimal?

**1. SDB regression: Hedley deglint + XGBoost — verdict: sound, no change warranted.**
Hedley et al. (2005, *Int. J. Remote Sensing* 26(10):2107–2112) **[verified]** is the standard glint-correction method, correctly applied. Published SDB comparisons show tree-ensemble methods (RF/XGBoost) generally match or beat classical log-ratio methods (Stumpf 2003, Lyzenga 1978) **[verified — both citations real]** when calibration data is adequate; your 1.35 m RMSE at 0–15 m is competitive with or better than typical published figures. The 15–25 m failure is a genuine physical optical-extinction limit, not a model-choice problem — no regression swap is expected to meaningfully close that gap. The one real opportunity: a physics-based or quantile-regression approach could give honest **per-pixel uncertainty** near the extinction depth, which is a worthwhile addition if the goal is better-labeled confidence, not a higher pass rate.

**2. ATL24 confidence threshold (0.6) — verdict: already best practice, keep as-is.**
This isn't an arbitrary cutoff: it's the exact value the ATL24 development team derived by maximizing classifier F-score against NOAA BlueTopo reference data, published in Parrish et al. 2025 and the companion Magruder et al. 2025 paper (both in *Earth & Space Science*, AGU) **[verified — both DOIs resolve to real papers matching the described methodology; this was the single riskiest citation in the whole research pass and it held up]**. Minor future refinement worth knowing about: the authors themselves suggest depth-band-specific thresholds could outperform one global cutoff — not worth implementing now, just a note for later.

**3. Priority-stack fusion — verdict: sound, matches real agency practice.**
NOAA's National Bathymetric Source program uses the same "best-available-source-wins, ranked by uncertainty/age" approach rather than blanket kriging for merging heterogeneous-accuracy bathymetry — kriging is used for reconciling overlapping soundings *within* one survey, a different problem than yours. **[verified via NOAA NBS documentation]** The one gap: NBS practice also emits a companion per-pixel source/uncertainty raster — your fusion already carries a `source` and `uncertainty` band per the pipeline docstring, so you're already doing this; just make sure it's actually populated and exposed, not just planned.

**4. Prospect layer (BPI + rugosity) — verdict: two concrete, citable improvements available.**
Your "rugosity" is a windowed elevation standard-deviation (TRI-like), but the actual Benthic Terrain Modeler standard metric is the **Vector Ruggedness Measure** (Sappington et al. 2007, *J. Wildlife Management* 71:1419–1426) **[verified]**, which decouples structural complexity from slope — a plain std-dev will flag a steep-but-smooth drop-off the same as a genuinely rugose reef. Worth swapping in `06_derivatives.py`. Separately, your BPI inner/outer radii (100 m/500 m) are notably larger than Lundblad et al.'s (2006) original BTM paper (fine-scale ~0–25 m, broad-scale ~10–100 m, for American Samoa reef features) **[verified]**, and a 2024 paper on BPI parameter optimization confirms outer-radius choice materially changes results and should track target feature scale. Worth testing a smaller radius pair (e.g. 30 m/150 m) alongside the current setting, since Sodwana's reef patches are plausibly smaller than the features that motivated the current defaults. The 92nd-percentile threshold itself has no clear literature precedent either way — not wrong, just not independently justified.

**5. Terrarium tile encoding — verdict: the diagnosed cause in your own Gotchas is probably wrong.**
Terrarium encodes elevation at 1/256 m ≈ 0.0039 m per step — finer than Mapbox's own Terrain-RGB (0.1 m/step) and finer than the "custom 0.01 m" fallback your Gotchas file proposes as a fix. **[verified against the Tilezen/Mapzen format spec]** If you're seeing visible terracing, switching encodings won't fix it, because the proposed fix is *coarser* than what you already have. More likely culprits: the underlying 10 m grid's real coarseness becoming visible under GPU color-relief interpolation, or PNG/tile resampling artifacts. Worth re-diagnosing by inspecting raw decoded pixel values along a slope transect before touching the encoding.

**6. Validation methodology — verdict: sound; don't describe results in IHO terms.**
Per-band RMSE against held-out (never-trained) ATL24 tracks is the right approach. Recomputing IHO S-44 Order 2 tolerances independently confirms: TVU ≈1.06 m at 15 m depth, ≈1.15 m at 25 m (a=1.00, b=0.023) **[verified — arithmetic checked]**. Your 1.35 m RMSE at 0–15 m already exceeds even the loosest IHO order, and 4.9–6.3 m at 15–25 m is 4–5× over it. That's expected and not a flaw — IHO S-44 governs certified hydrographic surveys, not opportunistic SDB — but it means you should keep reporting plain RMSE-by-band (as you already do) and avoid ever describing the map using IHO order language. IHO's own SDB guidance document, B-13 (March 2024) **[verified — real, current document]**, states best-case SDB accuracy as ≈0.5 m + 10–20% of depth, which is a more honest benchmark to cite if you want an external reference point.
